Looking Back: My Work in the LA Times' 1993 "Portraits From the Street" Review

A look back at press coverage from 1993. This post is our own recap and commentary, not a reprint of the original article — the full piece is linked below.

Long before I had a website at all, my photographs were part of a group show called "Intersecciones: el Trabajo, la Calle y el Documento" ("Intersections: Work, the Street and the Document") at McGroarty Arts Center in Tujunga — curated by Daniel Veneciano and reviewed by Nancy Kapitanoff in the Los Angeles Times on April 2, 1993.

The show put together several artists documenting the experience of recent Latino immigrants in Los Angeles: watercolor portraits of day laborers by Mary Heussenstamm, street-vendor photographs by Diego Cardoso and Lynn Flandera, a video documentary by Olivia Olea, and my own photographs and text on the INS's practice, through the 1980s, of using private property — vacant hotels among them — to detain and process immigrants.

The review mentions two bodies of my work specifically: images from the 1987 rally at the Mardi Gras Hotel near LAX (the same detention-center protest I've written about elsewhere on this site), and a series from a 1990 demonstration in downtown Los Angeles, where about 400 people protested the practice of detaining children to draw out their undocumented parents.

Reading this review again more than thirty years later, what stands out is how early this work started, and how long the same questions have stayed relevant. Read Nancy Kapitanoff's full 1993 review at the Los Angeles Times for the rest of the story, including the other artists in the show.

— Lisa
