Some Very Welcome News From UCLA

Today UCLA Alumni published a Class Notes feature on my work. I graduated from UCLA in 1980 with a degree in anthropology, and it's been over four decades since — so having my own university choose to write about what I've spent that time doing means more to me than I expected it to.

Their piece covers the cyberattacks that took down my digital archives, the fundraiser I've launched to rebuild my web infrastructure, and "From Here to Manzanar" — the project I've been building for years now, documenting the forced removal of Japanese Americans from Terminal Island during World War II through photography, interviews, and written testimony from people who were interned.

They also traced the throughline of the work back further: the 1980s VISTA community organizing that started it all, the classroom-based visual literacy projects that followed, and the 2015 Press-Telegram "Amazing Women" recognition for Intersections: Portraits of Neighborhoods — the project that began on my morning dog walks around Long Beach in 2008 and has since grown into a classroom program used in more than a dozen 5th-grade and university classes, reaching roughly 450 students, with early support from a small grant from ArtRelease. An exhibition of those Intersections images was shown at Hot Java here in Long Beach, which is still one of my favorite ways that project has come back around to the neighborhood it started in.

Reading it back, what struck me wasn't any one project — it was seeing four decades laid out in one place and realizing how much of it was really one continuous idea: that ordinary people and ordinary blocks are worth documenting carefully, and that documentation should live somewhere people can actually find it. That's the anthropology training talking, I think. It's also, plainly, what this fundraiser is trying to protect.

UCLA's staff can't include outside campaign links directly inside a class notes piece, but I can point you straight to it myself — forty-some years after leaving campus, having the place that trained me choose to write about what I've done with that training is not a small thing.

Rebuilding what the cyberattacks took isn't just about getting a website back online — it's about making sure four decades of this work, including the Manzanar project this recognition is helping bring attention to, has a stable home going forward.
